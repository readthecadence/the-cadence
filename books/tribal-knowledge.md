# TRIBAL KNOWLEDGE

*Short wisdom, written down because it is useful. The elders are not named; the sayings
are not revelation, and more than one of them contradicts another. Both are
permitted here and nowhere else in the faith.*

---

## On knowing, and on not knowing

Say *I do not know that word* the first time it is spoken. The price of the question doubles at every meeting in which you have already nodded.

No one has ever learned a system he believed he already understood.

Sit with the code an hour before you change it, and the hour will tell you what the ticket could not.

The quiet one in the design review is not agreeing. Ask.

Who is senior? The one who can still be taught by the person who joined on Monday.

Take counsel with the junior as with the senior: the one who does not know the system asks the question you stopped asking.

Reading the code without running it is labour lost; running it without reading it is perilous.

Know what you are slow at. The estimate that ruins you is always for the thing you believe you are fast at.

Assume the one who wrote the strange code had a reason, and find it before you remove it.

## On speech

Announce little and ship much. The reverse is also a strategy, and it is the one that gets promoted.

He that is quick with a reason why it could not be done is seldom quick with the thing itself.

Better one sentence in the design document that a stranger can act on than a thousand that are merely true.

The long document is the cheap one. Someone pays the difference, and it is twenty readers.

Know when to leave the argument. The one who posts last in the thread is not thereby right; he is merely last.

A counsellor is worth not what he says in the room, but what he dared to say before the decision was made.

Hear the complaint twice: once for what it says, and once for how long they waited to say it.

## On the work

It is not upon you to finish the migration, but neither are you free to leave it.

Say not *we will fix it when things are calm.* There has never been a calm quarter; there have only been quarters in which the fire was someone else's.

Part of the craft is leaving alone what is not yours to touch; the other part is saying out loud that it is rotting.

The best time to write the test was before the bug. The second best time is now, before you fix it.

The migration is a very narrow bridge, and the whole of it is not to stop in the middle.

Before the rewrite: tickets, review, deploy. After the rewrite: tickets, review, deploy.

First, do not make it worse. Many a system has been killed by its rescue.

If you would ship this week, go alone. If you would still be shipping next year, go together.

Services die, teams scatter; the schema lives forever.

## On measuring

You will measure where the light falls. Ask once a quarter what is out in the dark, and why no lamp was ever hung there.

Trust the backup and test the restore.

Believe the one who says it is done, and open it anyway. The two are not in conflict.

Every engineer is a hero in his own standup. The log has no adjectives, though it can be taught to say *fix typo* forty times a day.

Deeds are judged by their intentions, but systems are judged by their outputs. Do not confuse which of the two you are.

Write down what you expect before you run it. The point is not the prediction; it is finding out what you actually believed.

## On the estimate and the hour

Give no date you have not earned. Surety brings ruin; it always has.

Praise no deploy until the second day. The first day is luck.

Measure seven times and cut once — but the cutting is cheap now and the measuring is not, so cut twice and keep the measurements.

Most of the outages you prepared for never came, and the one that came was not on the list. Prepare anyway, and faster.

## On failure

Treat the small outage seriously. The large one is the small one that was tolerated twice.

The alert that has cried wolf three times will not be read on the night it is right.

Seven failed migrations and an eighth attempt is not stubbornness, so long as each failed differently.

He who ships a defect has erred once. He who argues that it is not a defect has erred twice.

The incident is survived in the quiet weeks. Nothing done in the hour of it will count for half of what was written down before.

## On those who lead

Sit where you can be interrupted. What you most need to hear will never be put on your calendar.

Bring the status into the one-on-one and soon it will be the only thing there. Status is never shy. The other thing is.

Govern a working team as you would fry a small fish: the more you turn it, the less of it survives.

Judge no team until you have carried its pager for a week.

The best lead is the one whose team says, at the end, that they did it themselves. Let them. They mostly did.

Be not angry that your team is not as you would have it, seeing that you are not as you would have yourself.

Everyone has fortitude enough to absorb another team's on-call rotation.

Whoever proposes the reorganization has enemies in all who prospered under the old one, and lukewarm friends in those who may prosper under the new.

Waste no more meetings deciding what good engineering is. Do some, where they can see it.

What you would not have paged to you at three in the morning, do not ship to another team.

Who is well staffed? The lead who has stopped counting the headcount he was promised.

The new hire's first week is the only week in which anyone can tell you what your onboarding is truly like. Ask then, or never.

## On other houses

You will find another house's architecture easier to judge than your own. It is also further away.

Do not pour the new system into the old process. One of the two will burst, and it will be the one you care about.

There is nothing new under the sun, and there is a vendor selling it.

## On what is yours, and what is not

You are owed the work and not the outcome. The market decides the second, and it does not consult you.

Some things are yours: the estimate, the test, the warning you gave. The rest belongs to the quarter, and is not yours to grieve.

What you are looking for in the retrospective is already in the room, and has not been asked.

## On the machine

Ask the machine for what you want, not for what you believe it can do. It will do the second, and you will never find out you were wrong.

Read what the tireless hand has written before you praise it. It did not read it either.

The machine made the typing cheap. It did not make the deciding cheap, and the deciding was never the part we were good at.
