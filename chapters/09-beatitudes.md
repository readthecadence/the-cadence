## IX. THE BEATITUDES

The Commandments say what the Aligned must *do*; the Beatitudes say who among them is *blessed*. It is told they were first spoken from a low stage with the slides gone dark and a clicker resting idle in the hand — the one keynote the faith reveres above all others. And the teaching was this:

> **1** Blessed are they who delete code, for they shall inherit a codebase that can be maintained.
>
> **2** Blessed are they who write it down, for they shall be understood in their absence.
>
> **3** Blessed are they who report the red, for they shall be mended while there is yet time.
>
> **4** Blessed are they who reach and fall short, for they have shown the height of the mountain.
>
> **5** Blessed are they who say *I was wrong* first, for they shall raise a house in which the truth is unafraid.
>
> **6** Blessed are they who say no, for their yes shall mean something, and the few things they keep shall be finished.
>
> **7** Blessed are they who ship, for the perfect thing left in the drawer has never once served a living soul.
>
> **8** Blessed are the paranoid, for they shall see the curve begin to bend while there is still time to turn upon it.
>

And the congregation, it is told, nodded gravely at every line — and then returned to their desks, and deleted nothing, and said no to no one, and reported the red as amber. For the blessings are easy to hear and hard to keep; and that, the elders say, is precisely why they are blessings, and not merely advice.
