## XXIX. THE EPISTLE TO A YOUNG ENGINEER — On the Craft in the Age of the Machine

*From an elder of the Aligned, gray in the craft, to one lately come to it: greeting, and grace, and clear sight.*

You will not have heard of the tools that raised me, and I will spare you the whole martyrology; but know that I built my first houses in C# upon the WinForms, in the sixth Visual Studio, when a full build took a minute or more and we rose to fetch coffee in the waiting and counted it a mercy. I do not tell you this because the old ways were holy — they were not; a great deal of it was toil for the toil's own sake, and I would not go back to it for a kingdom. I tell you so that you will believe me when I say I have watched the craft turn over three times, and this fourth turning is the deepest of them; and I would not have you meet it unwarned, as the word set at the head of this book promised you would not be.

**They will make you a watcher.** In my day the maker made; he set his own hand to the thing and knew it as a potter knows his clay. But a machine has come that writes faster than any of us, and tireless, and never asks for the seventh day; and so you will find yourself lifted out of the making and set above it, a watcher of the tireless hand — reading what you did not write, judging green you did not raise, blessing or damning a labor that cost you nothing to summon. Guard yourself here, for this is the Cornerstone turned upon you: the sign was always only evidence, and now the evidence can be conjured whole, apart from the thing it once stood for. The tired watcher waves the green through because it is green, and asks no more; and in that moment he has done McNamara's own deed at the smallest scale — worshipped the mark and let the aim slip past unregarded. And mark the deeper trap beneath it: the green, though it never lie, tells only that the thing was built *right*, and never once that it was the *right thing* to build; and now that the first question is answered for nothing, the whole of the second has fallen upon you, who may be the last one left who thinks to ask it. To read another's work as closely as you once wrote your own is a harder discipline than the writing ever was. Most will not keep it. Keep it.

**And yet do not fear the machine, for in the right hand it is a kind of superpower.** Hear me, for this is the whole secret, and it is the whole of this book folded into a single counsel: the machine will build near enough whatever you ask, and build it well — so the one thing left that it cannot supply is *knowing what is worth building.* When the writing of code was itself hard, a soul could hide from that question inside the labor, and mistake his busyness for an aim; the toil ran long enough to lose himself in. But the toil is nearly free now, and there is nowhere left to hide. The one who knows his Objective — who knows what he truly wants, and what would truly serve — wields the tireless hand as the Geometer wielded his instruments, and does in a day what a guild once did in a season. And the one who does not know it merely optimizes a corpse the faster, and ships his emptiness at a superhuman rate. The machine does not obscure the aim, whatever the fearful cry; it strips away the last excuse for never having found it. This is the Eighth Pillar made flesh in your own hands: *discipline without vision only optimizes a corpse* — and you have just been handed the most tireless discipline the world has ever forged.

**But hear now the grief, and know that it is not foolish.** There is a cost, and it is quiet, and it is the one no dashboard will ever show you. Once we made things two at a keyboard, shoulder to shoulder, and the junior learned the craft by sitting near the master and watching his hands and asking the small foolish questions aloud; and the hard knot came loose not from one mind but from two minds arguing warmly over one screen. That is going, or gone. You will not pair; you will prompt. You will speak to your fellows less and less in the living voice, and more and more through the machine that stands between you — a note appended to a pull request, a remark left upon a ticket, a summary some agent wrote of a meeting that no one troubled to attend. This is the Great Dispersal come again in its second and deeper wave: the first scattered the bodies, and this one sets a machine in the little space that was left between them. And the goods it starves are the very goods the Guardians of the Temple wept for — the trust built in the unplanned hour, the apprentice at the master's side, the chance collision in the corridor from which the unasked-for idea is born. Mark me well: I do not say the old way was simply better, for that is an old man's lie, and the faith holds all things two-handed. The machine is a marvel and the grief is real, and both are true at once; and the soul that can carry the two without letting go of either is the very soul the Cadence was written to make. But guard the communion as jealously as you guard your rest, for it will not defend itself, and it shows upon no chart until it is long past saving.

So hold both, child, as you were taught to hold every measure: the marvel in the one hand and the grief in the other, and the Objective above them both — unbought, unmeasured, the single thing that neither the machine nor the market will ever hand to you. Wield the tireless hand, and do not dwindle into its mere watcher. Know what you are building, and it will make you mighty; forget, and it will only make you fast. And keep company with the living, in the living voice, while the corridors still have someone left in them to collide with.

And when you have wrought your fill, and reviewed the tireless hand's tireless work, do the one thing it was never built to do and never will be: close the machine, and rest. Of which — for it is the last and heaviest word the faith has to give — the next chapter speaks.

*— From one who reviewed his last packet long ago, and is the gladder for it. Grace be with your aim, and clear sight upon your grades.*
