## ON CITATION

The verse-chapters of *The Cadence* may be cited by abbreviation and number, after the manner of the older scriptures: **Pil 1** is the First Pillar; **Cr 2**, the second line of the Creed; **KR 3**, the third Key Result; **Beat 8**, the eighth Beatitude; **Cat 12**, the twelfth question of the Catechism; **Par 5**, the fifth parable; **Hr 2**, the second prayer of the Hours. The abbreviations are **Pil** (the Nine Pillars), **Cr** (the Creed), **KR** (the Ten Key Results), **Beat** (the Beatitudes), **Cat** (the Catechism), **Par** (the Book of Parables), and **Hr** (the Book of Hours). The narrative and expository chapters carry no verses and are cited by name — as one cites a history or an epistle, and not a psalm.

## THE SAINTS AND KINGS HEREIN

*A key to the real people behind the myth — the fathers of the faith, under their worldly names. The lords of the present age keep their veils, and are not unmasked here.*

**The Kings** — *the charismatic builders of the Age of Kings.*
- **Sloan, the Temple-Builder** — Alfred P. Sloan, 1875–1966; who ran General Motors and drew the multidivisional blueprint every large company still lives inside.
- **Watson, the Giver of Culture** — Thomas J. Watson Sr., 1874–1956; founder-patriarch of IBM, who issued his people a creed, a uniform, and company songs they actually sang.
- **Ford, the Creator Who Would Not Turn** — Henry Ford, 1863–1947; father of the moving assembly line, who offered any color so long as it was black.
- **Iacocca, the Salesman-King** — Lee Iacocca, 1924–2019; who raised Chrysler from the grave on charisma and a record-selling memoir.
- **Welch, the Late King and Lawgiver of the Curve** — Jack Welch, 1935–2020, of General Electric; crowned "Manager of the Century," author of the rank-and-yank.
- **McNamara, the Tragedy of the Number** — Robert S. McNamara, 1916–2009; the brilliant Ford "Whiz Kid" and U.S. Secretary of Defense whose faith in the numbers helped run a war by the body count.

**The Prophets** — *the lineage of measurement.*
- **Taylor, the First Measurer** — Frederick Winslow Taylor, 1856–1915, engineer to the steel mills; father of "scientific management" and the man with the stopwatch.
- **Deming, the Prophet of Return** — W. Edwards Deming, 1900–1993, statistician; the quality prophet America ignored and postwar Japan revered.
- **Drucker, the Patriarch** — Peter F. Drucker, 1909–2005, Viennese émigré; who made management a humane discipline and gave it Management by Objectives.
- **Grove, the Great Prophet** — Andrew S. Grove, born Gróf András István, 1936–2016; the refugee who built Intel and wrote *High Output Management*.
- **Doerr, the Apostle** — John Doerr, b. 1951, venture capitalist; who carried Grove's OKRs to Google and wrote *Measure What Matters*.
- **Edmondson, the Prophetess** — Amy Edmondson of Harvard, who named psychological safety.
- **The People-Analysts, the Discerning Order** — the researchers behind Google's Project Oxygen and Project Aristotle, who measured what makes a manager and a team.

**The Mystic** — *the way of the unmeasured.*
- **Jobs, the Mystic** — Steve Jobs, 1955–2011, of Apple: cast out, returned, and canonized as the patron of taste over measurement.
- **The Woz** — Steve Wozniak, b. 1950; who built the first Apple machines by hand while Jobs saw the visions.
